What is Mechatronics?
The word Mechatronics is composed of "mecha" from mechanical and "tronics" from electronics.
Mechanical + Electronics = Mechatronics.
The term was coined by Tetsuro Mori, a senior Japanese engineer at Yasakawa Company in 1969.
Mechatronics is the synergic integration of mechanical engineering, electronics engineering, control engineering, and computer science.
A mechatronic system is a computer-controlled mechanical system.

1. Sensing:
   (a) Sensors
   (b) Signal Conditioning
   (c) Analog-to-Digital and Digital-to-Analog Conversion
2. Control:
   (a) Open Loop and Closed Loop Control
3. Action:
   (a) Drive Circuits
   (b) Actuators
   (c) Motors

Components of a Mechatronic System
- Mechanical, Electronics, Sensors, Actuators, Control, Computing.
- Mechanical System: A mechatronic system has at its core a mechanical system which needs to be commanded or controlled by a controller.
- Sensors: The controller needs information about the state of the system, obtained from sensors.
- Signal Conditioning: Signals produced by sensors often need conditioning to be read by the controller.
- ADC: Conditioned signals are converted to digital form by an Analog-to-Digital Convertor (ADC).
- Controller: The 'mind' of the mechatronic system, which processes user commands and sensed signals to generate command signals for actuators.
- Actuators: Devices that can convert electrical energy to mechanical energy.

Basic Elements of Control System
A control system consists of several elements where each performs a particular task, connected in sequence to facilitate signal flow.
Main types of control systems:
A. Open loop control system
B. Closed loop control system

Open Loop Control System
- The output is dependent on the input, but the input or controlling action is independent of the output or change in the output (no feedback).
- Advantages: Relatively simple, low cost, easy to understand, stability and reliability are relatively good.
- Disadvantages: Inaccuracy, slow in response, no optimized control. Requires a good calibration between the input and output.

Closed Loop System
- The command signals sent to the actuators utilize feedback information from the sensors.
- Compensates for disturbances by measuring the output response, feeding that measurement back through a feedback path, and comparing it to the input at a summing junction.
- Advantages: Greater accuracy than open-loop systems, less sensitive to noise, disturbances, and changes in the environment. Does not require calibration information.

Applications
- Smart consumer products: Home security, cameras, microwave ovens, dishwashers, climate control units.
- Medical: Implant devices, assisted surgery.
- Defence: Unmanned air, ground, and underwater vehicles, jet engines.
- Manufacturing: Robotics, machines, processes.
- Automotive: Climate control, antilock brakes, active suspension, cruise control, airbags, engine management.
