Energy and Global Warming

Syllabus
Energy: Introduction and applications of Energy sources like Fossil fuels, nuclear fuels, Hydel, Solar, wind, and bio-fuels, Environmental issues like Global warming and Ozone depletion.

Introduction to Energy
- Definition: Energy is the capacity to do work. It is essential for all human activities, from running machines to supporting life processes.
- Forms of Energy: Mechanical, thermal, chemical, electrical, nuclear, etc.
- Types of Energy Sources:
  - Non-Renewable: Fossil fuels, nuclear fuels.
  - Renewable: Solar, wind, hydropower, biofuels.

Applications of Energy Sources

1. Fossil Fuels
- Description: Includes coal, oil, and natural gas, formed from ancient organic matter over millions of years.
- Applications: Power generation in thermal power plants, fuel for transportation (petrol, diesel), industrial heating and production.
- Limitations: Non-renewable and finite. Contributes heavily to pollution and greenhouse gas emissions.

2. Nuclear Fuels
- Description: Uses uranium or thorium to generate energy through nuclear fission.
- Applications: Electricity generation in nuclear power plants, medical uses (radiation therapy), research and defense applications.
- Limitations: High initial costs and risks of radiation. Produces long-lived radioactive waste.

3. Hydropower (Hydel Energy)
- Description: Uses the potential energy of water stored in dams or flowing rivers.
- Applications: Electricity generation in hydro-electric power plants, water supply and irrigation.
- Limitations: Environmental impact on aquatic ecosystems. High cost of dam construction.

4. Solar Energy
- Description: Captures sunlight to generate electricity or heat.
- Applications: Solar panels for electricity, solar water heaters and cookers.
- Advantages: Clean, renewable, and widely available.
- Challenges: Dependency on sunlight and high setup costs.

5. Wind Energy
- Description: Converts kinetic energy from wind into electricity using wind turbines.
- Applications: Wind farms for large-scale power generation.
- Advantages: Renewable and produces no direct emissions.
- Challenges: Requires windy locations and large land areas.

6. Biofuels
- Description: Derived from organic materials like plants and animal waste.
- Applications: Alternative fuel for vehicles (ethanol, biodiesel), heat and electricity generation.
- Advantages: Renewable and reduces waste.
- Challenges: Can compete with food production.

Environmental Issues

Global Warming
- Definition: The gradual increase in Earth's average surface temperature due to the buildup of greenhouse gases like carbon dioxide, methane, and nitrous oxide.
- Causes: Burning of fossil fuels, deforestation, and industrial activities.
- Effects: Melting polar ice caps, rising sea levels, extreme weather patterns, and biodiversity loss.
- Mitigation: Transition to renewable energy sources, enhancing energy efficiency, and reforestation.

Ozone Layer Depletion
- Definition: Thinning of the ozone layer in the stratosphere, primarily caused by chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs) and other ozone-depleting substances.
- Importance of Ozone Layer: Protects Earth from harmful ultraviolet (UV) radiation.
- Effects of Depletion: Increased UV radiation leading to skin cancer and cataracts. Damage to ecosystems and marine life.
- Mitigation: Phasing out ozone-depleting substances and promoting alternatives to CFCs.
