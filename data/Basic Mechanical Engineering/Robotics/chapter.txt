Module IV
Robotics

Syllabus
Introduction to Mechatronics and Robotics: open-loop and closed-loop mechatronic systems. Classification based on robotics configuration: polar cylindrical, Cartesian coordinate and spherical. Application, Advantages and disadvantages.

What is a Robot?
"A Robot is a programmable, multi-function manipulator designed to move material, parts, tools or special devices through variable programmed motions for performance of variety of tasks."

Why Robots?
- Economical and practical.
- Hazardous and uncomfortable working conditions (e.g., spray painting chambers).
- Repetitive tasks (e.g., pick and place operations, machine loading and unloading).
- Difficult handling (e.g., heavy work parts).
- Multishift operations: Robots can work 24x7.

Selecting Right Applications
Criteria for finding practical and economic robot applications:
- Simple repetitive operations.
- Cycle times are greater than 5 seconds.
- Parts can be delivered in proper location and orientation.
- No inspection required.
- Setups and changeovers are not frequent.

Applications of Robots
- Material handling and transfer: Moving work parts, simple pick and place, transfer between conveyors, palletizing, stacking.
- Machine loading/unloading: Supplying a production machine with raw parts or unloading finished parts (e.g., Die casting, injection molding, hot forging, stamping).
- Welding: Spot welding (localized) and Arc welding (continuous). Advantages: Higher productivity, improved safety, more consistent welds.
- Spray coating/painting: Protects humans from fumes, mist, noise, fire hazards. Advantages: Safety, coating consistency, lower material usage, greater productivity.
- Assembly and inspection: Used in assembly plants due to programmability and adaptability. Inspection using mechanical probes or optical sensing capabilities.
- Machining.

Elements of a Robot System
A Robot is a system consisting of the following integrated elements:
1. Manipulator / Rover: This is the main body of the robot and consists of links, joints, and structural elements.
2. End Effector: The part that generally handles objects, makes connections to other machines, or performs the required tasks (e.g., a gripper or a spray nozzle).
3. Actuators: The 'muscles' of the manipulators. Common types are servomotors, stepper motors, and pneumatic cylinders.
4. Sensors: Used to collect information about the internal state of the robot or to communicate with the outside environment (e.g., vision systems, touch sensors).
5. Controller: Receives data from the computer, controls the motions of the actuator, and coordinates these motions with the sensory feedback information.

Robot Configurations (Kinematic Configurations)
Specifies the possible movements provided by different robots:
1. Cartesian Manipulator:
   - Manipulator that can move in x, y, and z directions.
   - Simple design.
   - Also known as Gantry robots (similar to overhead gantry cranes).
   - Disadvantage: An application must lie "inside" the robot. Structure limits the size and placement of fixtures and sensors.
   - Applications: Assembly plants.
2. Cylindrical Manipulator: Consists of a vertical column and a slide that can be moved up and down along the column.
3. Spherical Manipulator: Uses polar coordinates. Arm can pivot and rotate to cover a spherical work envelope.
4. SCARA (Selective Compliance Assembly Robot Arm): They have two revolute joints that are parallel and allow the robot to move in a horizontal plane, plus an additional prismatic joint that moves vertically.
5. Articulated Robot.

Control Loops of Robotic System
- Open-loop control systems: The output has no effect upon the input. Example: A constant voltage applied to an electric motor. If the load changes, the shaft speed decreases, but the input voltage remains unaffected by the speed variation.
- Closed-loop control systems: Outputs affect the input to the controlled element. Output is sensed and fed back to be compared with input variables. Example: Motor speed is measured with a tachometer and compared with the input voltage; corrective actions are taken to adjust the input voltage.

Wrist Motions
- Roll (R) axis: Involves rotation of the wrist mechanism about the arm axis.
- Pitch (P) axis: Involves up or down rotation of the wrist.
- Yaw (Y) axis: Involves right or left rotation of the wrist.
