Module III
Welding and Joining Processes

Syllabus
Joining Processes: Soldering, Brazing and Welding, Definitions, classification of welding process, Arc welding, Gas welding and types of flames.

Manufacturing Processes
Manufacturing is the process of converting raw materials into finished goods to meet customer requirements. The joining process is one of the key manufacturing methods alongside shaping, machining, and surface finishing.

Welding
- Definition: Welding is a process of making a permanent joint by establishing inter-atomic bonds between two or more pieces of metal by using heat or heat and pressure.
- Essential Requirements: Heat is the most essential requirement. Pressure, filler material, and flux (used to resist oxidation and remove impurities) may or may not be used.

Classification of Welding:
1. Plastic Welding (Pressure Welding):
   - The metals to be joined are heated to a plastic state and then forced together by external pressure.
   - No filler material is added.
   - Examples: Forge welding, Resistance welding.
2. Fusion Welding (Diffusion Welding):
   - No pressure is involved. A very high temperature is produced at the joint.
   - The metal is heated to the molten state, and a joint forms upon solidification.
   - A filler material may be used. Heat may be generated by an electric arc, gas combustion, or chemical action.
   - Examples: Arc welding, Gas welding.

Advantages of Welding:
- Low cost of arrangement.
- Automation is highly possible (e.g., robotic arms).
- Resistance to corrosion.
- Portability (small-sized equipment available).
- Can weld a large variety of metals, aiding mass production.

Disadvantages of Welding:
- Workpiece can deform during the operation.
- Heat treatment is often required to refine grain structure and improve mechanical properties.
- Produces harmful high-frequency radiations and gases.
- Requires skilled labor.

Applications of Welding:
- Aircraft and automobile construction (chassis, body).
- Bridges, buildings, and railways.
- Pressure vessels, tanks, and pipelines.
- Shipbuilding and heavy machineries.

Gas Welding
- A fusion-welding process that joins metals using the heat of combustion of a fuel gas (acetylene, hydrogen, natural gas) in air or oxygen.
- Temperature ranges from 3000°C to 3400°C.
- Used particularly for thin sections up to 6 mm thick.
- Types: Oxy-Acetylene, Air-Acetylene, Oxy-Hydrogen.

Oxy-Acetylene Welding Equipment:
- Acetylene and Oxygen Cylinders.
- Regulators: Reduce gas pressures and produce a steady flow.
- Filler Rods: Provide filler metal.
- Flux: Removes impurities and oxides.
- Welding Torch (Blow Pipe): Mixes oxygen and fuel gas and controls the flow to the nozzle.

Types of Flame in Oxy-Acetylene Welding:
1. Neutral Flame: Equal volume of acetylene and oxygen. Temp ~3260°C. Has a sharp inner cone and outer envelope. Used for mild steel, stainless steel, aluminum, copper, and cast iron.
2. Oxidizing Flame: More oxygen in proportion. Highest temperature ~3482°C. Pointed, shorter inner cone. Used for brass and copper-based alloys. Not used for steels as excess oxygen oxidizes the metal.
3. Carburizing (Reducing) Flame: More acetylene. Temp ~3037°C. Has three zones (inner cone, whitish intermediate feather, blue outer cone). Used for low alloy steels and surface hardening.

Advantages of Gas Welding:
- Easily controlled flame, suitable for thin sheets, highly portable, low cost, versatile (welding, heating, cutting).
Disadvantages of Gas Welding:
- Cannot join heavy sections, slower than arc welding, lower flame temperature than arc, fluxes produce irritating fumes, more distortion compared to arc welding.

Arc Welding
- A method of fusion welding where heat is generated by an electric arc between an anode (electrode) and a cathode (base metal).
- Temperature of the arc is about 6000°C to 7000°C.
- Equipment: Transformer/Generator, electrode holder, cables, safety tools.

Electrodes:
- Non-consumable: Carbon, tungsten, or graphite. Do not melt during welding.
- Consumable: Melts during welding to act as filler. Can be bare or flux-coated.

Manual Metal Arc Welding (Shielded Metal Arc Welding - SMAW):
- Electrode movement is manually controlled.
- Shielding is provided by a slag formed over the weld pool and inactive gases generated through the thermal decomposition of the flux coating on the electrode.

Welding Defects:
- Weld crack, Porosity, Undercut, Spatter, Slag inclusion, Poor fusion, Warping and distortion, Lack of penetration.

Soldering
- A process where items are joined by melting a filler metal (Solder: Lead-tin alloy) into the joint.
- Melting temperature is below 450°C and lower than the melting point of the base metals (base metals do not melt).
- The filler metal flows via capillary action.
- Types: Soft soldering (150-300°C) for non-load bearing parts. Hard soldering (silver alloy with tin, 300-600°C) for stronger joints.
- Applications: Electronics industry (PCBs), copper, silver, gold.

Brazing
- Joins metals by melting and flowing a filler metal (Spelter: copper-zinc alloy) into the joint via capillary action.
- Melting temperature is above 450°C but lower than the base metals.
- Provides a much stronger joint compared to soldering.
- Types: Torch, Furnace, Resistance, Inductive brazing.
- Applications: Pipe fittings, radiators, heat exchangers.
