1. ADVANCED ROLE OF CFO IN VARIOUS MATTERS INCLUDING VALUE CREATION
================================================================================
Traditionally, the primary role of the Chief Financial Officer (CFO) was concentrated on shareholder wealth maximisation by managing the financial health of the organisation, implementing accounting standards, and overseeing internal financial controls.

In the post-pandemic, globalised, and tech-driven era, the CFO's role has expanded drastically from a financial gatekeeper into a strategic business partner and leader in sustainable Value Creation:
a. Risk Management:
   CFOs now oversee the comprehensive Enterprise Risk Management (ERM) framework, identifying strategic, operational, financial, geopolitical, and cyber risks.
b. Supply Chain Management:
   Post-pandemic disruptions make supply chains a critical determinant of financial viability and continuous operations. CFOs evaluate financial risks and cost-benefit trade-offs across supplier networks.
c. Mergers, Acquisitions, and Corporate Restructuring (M&A):
   Strategic decisions regarding inorganic growth, valuation, due diligence, synergy realization, and capital restructuring fall directly under the CFO's purview. Errors in M&A can lead to business failure.
d. Environmental, Social, and Governance (ESG) Financing:
   CFOs lead the transition from traditional financing to green/sustainable financing (green bonds, sustainability-linked loans, ESG reporting, carbon credits).

2. STRATEGIC FINANCIAL DECISION-MAKING FRAMEWORK
================================================================================
Capital investment is the springboard for wealth creation. Capital is the limiting factor; hence, management faces the strategic challenge of allocating limited funds between competing uses.

Fundamentals of Business Equation:
Strategy + Finance + Management = Fundamentals of Business

Core Decisions of Strategic Financial Management (SFM):
1. Financing Decisions: Determining the optimal capital structure—mix of equity capital and debt capital to minimise Weighted Average Cost of Capital (WACC).
2. Investment Decisions (Capital Budgeting): Allocating funds to long-term capital projects evaluated against expected risk and return.
3. Dividend Decisions: Deciding the optimal division of earnings between dividend distribution and profit retention for reinvestment.
4. Portfolio Decisions: Evaluating investments on their contribution to aggregate corporate portfolio performance rather than standalone risk.

SFM combines the backward-looking, report-focused discipline of financial accounting with the dynamic, forward-looking discipline of strategic financial management to maximize enterprise value.

3. STRATEGY AT DIFFERENT HIERARCHY LEVELS
================================================================================
Strategies operate across three organisational levels:

1. Corporate Level Strategy:
   Concerned with the overall scope of the firm and portfolio of businesses. It answers three fundamental questions:
   • Suitability: Whether the strategy accomplishes the firm's core long-term objectives.
   • Feasibility: Determines resource requirements (financial, human, technological) to formulate and execute the strategy.
   • Acceptability: Assesses stakeholders' financial and non-financial satisfaction (returns, risk, ESG).

2. Business Unit Level Strategy:
   Focuses on Strategic Business Units (SBUs)—autonomous profit centres. The focus is on building and sustaining competitive advantage (cost leadership, differentiation).

3. Functional Level Strategy:
   Operating departments (R&D, Operations, Marketing, HR, Finance). Finance assumes the greatest importance as the bridge for resource mobilization and allocation.

4. FINANCIAL PLANNING
================================================================================
Financial planning defines the overall operational and financial feasibility of business activities.

Three Major Components of Financial Planning:
Financial Planning = Financial Resources (FR) + Financial Tools (FT) + Financial Goals (FG)

Outcomes of Financial Planning:
• Financial Objectives: Defined at the outset, aligning with corporate mission.
• Financial Decision-Making: Analyzing corporate financial bottlenecks and selecting courses of action.
• Financial Performance Measures: Ratio analysis (ROCE, ROE), cash flow statements, and Economic Value Added (EVA).

5. INTERFACE OF FINANCIAL POLICY AND STRATEGIC MANAGEMENT
================================================================================
Money is both the starting point and the ending point of an enterprise.

Key Dimensions of the Interface:
1. Sources of Finance and Capital Structure:
   • Internal mobilization (retained earnings, depreciation funds) vs External mobilization (equity shares, preference shares, debentures, public deposits, bank credit).
   • Capital Structure Norms:
     - Public Sector Enterprises: Ideal Debt-Equity norm is 1:1.
     - Private Sector Enterprises: Ideal Debt-Equity norm is 2:1 (higher for capital-intensive sectors).

2. Investment & Fund Allocation Decisions:
   Three types of investment proposals evaluated through capital budgeting:
   a. Addition of a new product.
   b. Expansion of capacity of existing products (plant expansion or new facility).
   c. Cost reduction and process modernization.

3. Dividend Policy:
   Determines the trade-off between dividend payout and retained earnings for funding growth:
   • Stable dividend policy (enhances share market price).
   • Constant payout ratio (maintains financial flexibility).
   • Minimum base dividend plus extra dividend during high-earning years.

6. BALANCING FINANCIAL GOALS VIS-À-VIS SUSTAINABLE GROWTH
================================================================================
Financial Sustainability Criteria (CIVICUS Framework):
To be financially sustainable, an organisation must:
1. Have more than one source of income.
2. Have more than one way of generating income.
3. Conduct strategic, action, and financial planning regularly.
4. Maintain adequate internal financial systems and controls.
5. Possess a good public image and clear corporate values.
6. Enjoy financial autonomy.

Sustainable Growth Rate (SGR) - Robert C. Higgins Model:
SGR represents the maximum annual percentage increase in sales that can be achieved without issuing new equity, given the firm's operating efficiency, asset utilization, dividend payout, and debt leverage policies.

Formula:
SGR = ROE × (1 - b)
Where:
• ROE = Return on Equity = PAT / Net Worth
• b = Dividend Payout Ratio (d)
• (1 - b) = Retention Ratio = 1 - Dividend Payout Ratio

Assumptions of Sustainable Growth Model:
1. The firm maintains a target capital structure without issuing new equity.
2. The firm maintains a target dividend payout ratio.
3. Sales grow as rapidly as market conditions allow.
4. Total Assets and sales maintain a stable proportional relationship.

Twin Cornerstones of Sustainable Growth:
• Growth Strategy: Market positioning and sales expansion goals.
• Growth Capability: Operational and financial infrastructure to execute the strategy.

Impact of Inflation on SGR:
Inflation inflates the nominal monetary cost of replacing assets. Because creditors mandate maintaining historical-cost debt-equity ratios, inflation demands additional financing and lowers real sustainable growth.

7. EXTERNAL FUNDING REQUIREMENT (EFR)
================================================================================
When a company plans a sales growth rate exceeding its Sustainable Growth Rate, it incurs an External Funding Requirement (EFR).

Approaches to Compute EFR:
1. Analytical Approach:
   Balance sheet items (Current Assets, Current Liabilities, Fixed Assets) are projected in proportion to sales expansion, and the net shortfall between required assets and internally generated funds (retained profit) is calculated.

2. Formula-Based Approach:
   EFR = [ (TA / S) × ΔS ] - [ (P / S) × ΔS ] - [ N × S1 × (1 - d) ]
   Where:
   • TA = Total Assets at baseline
   • S = Current baseline sales
   • ΔS = Projected change in sales (S1 - S)
   • S1 = Projected revised sales
   • P = Spontaneous payables and current liabilities that grow with sales
   • N = Net Profit Margin Ratio (PAT / Sales)
   • d = Dividend Payout Ratio
   • (1 - d) = Earnings Retention Ratio

Allocation of EFR:
After computing total EFR, management determines the portion to be raised from:
• Short-term loans (based on current ratio or working capital policies)
• Long-term debt (constrained by Fixed Asset to Long-term loan ratios)
• Fresh Equity issue (residual amount required to keep Debt-Equity ratio within acceptable ceilings)
