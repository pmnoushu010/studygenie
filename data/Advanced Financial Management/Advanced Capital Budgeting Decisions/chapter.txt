1. CURRENT TRENDS IN CAPITAL BUDGETING
================================================================================
Traditional capital budgeting models assume cash flows are known with certainty. In practical corporate finance, investment proposals operate under dynamic real-world forces:

1.1 Impact of Inflation on Capital Budgeting:
Inflation increases project revenues and operating costs over time. However, it introduces significant distortions because:
• Depreciation is based on Historical Cost: Tax shields from depreciation do not adjust with inflation.
  $$\text{Annual Cash Inflow After Tax} = (R - C - D)(1 - T) + D = (R - C)(1 - T) + DT$$
  While revenues ($R$) and costs ($C$) inflate, the depreciation tax shield ($DT$) remains fixed in nominal terms, eroding the project's real rate of return.
• Consistency Rule in Inflation:
  - Discount Nominal Cash Flows at Nominal Discount Rate.
  - Discount Real Cash Flows at Real Discount Rate.
  - Fisher Relation:
    $$(1 + \text{Nominal Discount Rate}) = (1 + \text{Real Discount Rate}) \times (1 + \text{Inflation Rate})$$
    $$\text{Real Discount Rate} = \frac{1 + \text{Nominal Discount Rate}}{1 + \text{Inflation Rate}} - 1$$
• Differential Inflation Rates:
  When inflation rates for revenues ($i_r$) and costs ($i_c$) differ:
  $$\text{NPV} = \sum_{t=1}^{n} \frac{[R_t(1+i_r)^t - C_t(1+i_c)^t](1-T) + D_t T}{(1 + k)^t} - I_0$$

1.2 Impact of Change in Technology:
Technological obsolescence shortens product lifecycles, alters manufacturing processes, and may require mid-project capital modernization. Techniques like Sensitivity Analysis, Simulation, and Project Abandonment Options incorporate technological risk.

1.3 Impact of Change in Government Policies:
• Fiscal Policy: Adjustments in corporate tax rates, depreciation write-offs, and Double Tax Avoidance Agreements (DTAA).
• Monetary Policy: Changes in Repo rate and statutory reserve ratios (CRR/SLR) impacting the cost of debt ($K_d$) and working capital financing.

2. DEALING WITH RISK IN INVESTMENT DECISIONS
================================================================================
Decision-Making Environments:
1. Decision Making under Certainty: Future cash flows are completely known.
2. Decision Making involving Risk: Future cash flows are variable, but an objective/subjective probability distribution can be assigned.
3. Decision Making under Uncertainty: Future cash flows cannot be assigned probabilities.

Reasons for Adjusting Risk:
• Opportunity Cost: Capital invested in risky projects demands a risk premium matching comparable risk investments.
• Preserving Enterprise Value: Failure to account for risk leads to overvaluation of volatile projects and potential corporate insolvency.

================================================================================
3. INTERNAL AND EXTERNAL FACTORS AFFECTING CAPITAL BUDGETING
================================================================================
A. Internal Factors:
1. Project-Specific Risk: Risks unique to a specific project (e.g., resource estimation errors, commissioning delays, technology debugging).
2. Company-Specific Risk: Risks affecting the overall enterprise (e.g., credit rating downgrades, key personnel departures, labor strikes, IPR litigations).

B. External Factors:
1. Industry-Specific Risk: Regulatory mandates, technological disruptions, or environmental restrictions affecting an entire industry sector.
2. Market Risk: Shifts in consumer demand, introduction of substitute products, or raw material supply constraints.
3. Competition Risk: Entry of aggressive rivals, price wars, or product obsolescence.
4. Economic Risk: Macroeconomic changes in GDP growth, inflation, interest rates, and foreign exchange rates.
5. International Risk: Trade embargoes, cross-border tariff barriers, political unrest, or changes in bilateral treaties.

4. METHODS OF INCORPORATING RISK IN CAPITAL BUDGETING
================================================================================

4.1 Statistical Techniques:
• Expected Net Cash Flow (ENCF):
  $$\text{ENCF} = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \text{NCF}_i \times P_i$$
• Expected Net Present Value (ENPV):
  $$\text{ENPV} = \sum_{t=1}^{n} \frac{\text{ENCF}_t}{(1 + k)^t} - I_0$$
• Variance ($\sigma^2$):
  $$\sigma^2 = \sum_{j=1}^{n} (\text{NCF}_j - \text{ENCF})^2 \times P_j$$
• Standard Deviation ($\sigma$): Measure of absolute risk / dispersion: $\sigma = \sqrt{\sigma^2}$.
• Coefficient of Variation (CV): Measure of relative risk per unit of return:
  $$\text{CV} = \frac{\text{Standard Deviation}}{\text{Expected Value (Mean)}} = \frac{\sigma}{\text{ENCF or ENPV}}$$
  Decision Rule: When evaluating projects of unequal sizes, select the project with the lower Coefficient of Variation.

4.2 Conventional Techniques:
1. Risk-Adjusted Discount Rate (RADR):
   $$\text{RADR} = \text{Risk-Free Rate } (R_f) + \text{Risk Premium } (R_p)$$
   $$\text{NPV} = \sum_{t=1}^{n} \frac{\text{NCF}_t}{(1 + \text{RADR})^t} - I_0$$
   Advantage: Easy to understand and apply.
   Limitation: Assumes risk compounds over time at a constant rate.

2. Certainty Equivalent (CE) Approach:
   Converts risky future cash flows into equivalent certain (risk-free) cash flows using Certainty Equivalent coefficients ($\alpha_t$ where $0 \le \alpha_t \le 1$):
   $$\alpha_t = \frac{\text{Certain Cash Flow}_t}{\text{Risky Cash Flow}_t}$$
   $$\text{NPV} = \sum_{t=1}^{n} \frac{\alpha_t \times \text{NCF}_t}{(1 + k_f)^t} - I_0$$
   CRITICAL PRINCIPLE: Discounting MUST be done using the Risk-Free Rate ($k_f$). Using the cost of capital would cause double counting of risk (once in the numerator via $\alpha_t$, and again in the denominator).

4.3 Advanced Modelling Techniques:
1. Sensitivity Analysis:
   Evaluates how much the project's NPV (or IRR) changes in response to an adverse variation in a SINGLE input variable (e.g., selling price, variable cost, fixed cost, sales volume, initial capital cost) while holding all other variables constant. Identifies the most critical variable.
2. Scenario Analysis:
   Examines the impact of simultaneous changes in multiple variables across predefined economic states (e.g., Worst Case, Most Likely Case, Best Case).
3. Monte Carlo Simulation Analysis:
   Constructs a mathematical model of project cash flows, assigns probability distributions to stochastic variables (annual sales, operating life), generates random numbers, runs hundreds of iterations, and computes the probability distribution of NPVs.
4. Decision Tree Analysis:
   Graphic representation of sequential, multi-period investment decisions over time:
   • Decision Nodes (Squares $\square$): Management has control over the choice; select the alternative with the highest Expected Monetary Value (EMV).
   • Chance/Event Nodes (Circles $\bigcirc$): Outcomes governed by probabilities; compute expected value across all radiating branches.
   • Rollback Procedure: Evaluated from right to left (terminal outcomes to inception).

5. REPLACEMENT DECISIONS
================================================================================
5.1 Replacement of Existing Machine (Incremental Analysis):
When evaluating whether to replace an existing asset with a newer machine:
• Step 1: Initial Net Cash Outflow ($CF_0$):
  Cost of New Machine + Installation Costs + Additional Working Capital
  Less: Net Salvage Value of Old Machine [Gross Resale Value $\pm$ Tax Benefit/Liability on Loss/Gain on Sale].
  $$\text{Tax on Sale of Old Machine} = (\text{Book Value} - \text{Market Value}) \times \text{Tax Rate}$$
• Step 2: Incremental Operating Cash Inflows ($\Delta \text{CFAT}$):
  $$\Delta \text{CFAT} = [(\Delta \text{Sales} \pm \Delta \text{Costs}) - \Delta \text{Depreciation}](1 - T) + \Delta \text{Depreciation}$$
• Step 3: Terminal Inflow: Net salvage value of new machine plus release of working capital.
• Step 4: Decision: Accept if Incremental NPV $> 0$.

5.2 Optimum Replacement Cycle (Equivalent Annual Cost - EAC):
When a machine requires periodic, repeating replacement over an ongoing horizon:
As an asset ages, maintenance and repair expenses escalate, while resale/scrap values decline.
• To compare replacement cycles of unequal duration (e.g., 1 year, 2 years, 3 years, 4 years), compute the Equivalent Annual Cost (EAC):
  $$\text{EAC} = \frac{\text{Present Value of Net Cash Outflows}}{\text{PVIFA}(k, n)}$$
• Decision Rule: The optimum replacement cycle is the period that produces the MINIMUM Equivalent Annual Cost (EAC).

6. ADJUSTED PRESENT VALUE (APV)
================================================================================
Traditional NPV discounts cash flows at WACC, which assumes a constant debt-equity ratio.
The Adjusted Present Value (APV) method separates the investment decision from the financing decision:
$$\text{APV} = \text{Base Case NPV} + \text{PV of Financing Side Effects}$$
Where:
• Base Case NPV: NPV of the project assuming it is 100% equity-financed (discounted at the unlevered cost of equity $K_e^u$).
• PV of Financing Effects: Primarily includes the Present Value of Tax Shields on debt interest:
  $$\text{PV of Interest Tax Shield} = \sum \frac{\text{Interest Payment} \times \text{Tax Rate}}{(1 + K_d)^t}$$
• Other side effects: Subsidized loans, flotation costs, and financial distress costs.
• Decision Rule: Accept the project if $\text{APV} > 0$.
