1. INTRODUCTION & WORKING PRINCIPLE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A Diesel Power Plant employs a heavy-duty compression-ignition internal combustion diesel engine coupled directly to an AC generator (alternator) to generate electrical power.

Basic Working Principle:
• When the diesel fuel burning inside the engine, its start to produce a mechanical power.
• The combustion of diesel fuel produces increased temperature & pressure inside the engine.
• The combustion temperature of the burning fuel inside the engine cylinder is 1500°C to 2000°C.
• Due to this pressure, gases are formed; this gas pushes the piston inside the diesel engine, and then mechanical power is produced.
• With the use of this mechanical power, the shaft of diesel engine starts rotating.
• The rotating shaft turns the rotor of the directly coupled AC synchronous generator (alternator) to produce electricity.


2. ESSENTIAL SYSTEMS OF DIESEL POWER PLANT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A complete diesel power plant includes the following major systems and circuits:

(i) Fuel Supply System:
    • Fuel from the storage tank is pumped through a filter into a smaller tank called all-day tank.
    • This tank supplies the daily requirements of the diesel engine by gravity head.
    • A fuel overflow pipe returns excess pumped fuel safely back to the bulk storage tank.
    • High-pressure fuel injection pump (FIP) delivers metered fuel to fuel injectors located in cylinder heads.

(ii) Air Intake System:
    • The air required for the combustion of fuel inside the diesel engine cylinder is drawn through the air filter.
    • The purpose of the filter is to remove dust from the incoming air to prevent abrasive wear on cylinder liners and piston rings.

(iii) Exhaust System:
    • The exhaust gases coming out of the engine is very noisy.
    • In order to reduce the noise, a silencer is used in the exhaust line before releasing gases into the atmosphere.

(iv) Cooling System:
    • The temperature of the burning fuel inside the engine cylinder is 1500°C to 2000°C.
    • In order to lower this temperature, water is circulated around the engine jackets.
    • The hot water leaving the jacket is passed through the heat exchanger.
    • The heat from the heat exchanger is carried away by the raw water circulated through the heat exchanger and is cooled in the cooling tower for recirculation.
    • An expansion surge tank accommodates water expansion and maintains positive suction head.

(v) Lubricating System:
    • This circuit includes lubricating oil tank, oil pump, and oil cooler.
    • The purpose of the lubrication system is to reduce the wear of the engine moving parts (parts of the cylinder such as piston, shafts, valves must be lubricated).
    • Lubrication also helps to cool the engine by absorbing frictional heat.

(vi) Engine Starting System:
    • Large industrial diesel engines use a Compressed Air Starting System:
      - Air compressor pressurizes air to 20 to 30 bar.
      - Air tank / receiver stores starting air.
      - High-pressure air admitted into cylinders spins crankshaft to firing speed.

(vii) AC Generator (Alternator):
    • Converts the rotational mechanical power of the diesel engine into electrical power.


3. ADVANTAGES AND DISADVANTAGES OF DIESEL POWER PLANT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Advantages:
1. Very simple design also simple installation and occupies less space.
2. Limited cooling water requirement.
3. Diesel power plant are more efficient than steam power in the range of 150 MW capacity.
4. Quickly started and put on load.
5. It can respond to varying loads without any difficulty.
6. Smaller storage is needed for the fuel.
7. Layout of power plant is quite simple.
8. There is no problem of ash handling.
9. Less supervision required.

Disadvantages:
1. High Maintenance, lubrication cost and operating cost.
2. Fuel cost is more, since in India diesel is costly.
3. The plant cost per kW is comparatively more.
4. The life of diesel power plant is small due to high maintenance.
5. Noise is a serious problem in diesel power plant.
6. Diesel power plant cannot be constructed for large scale.


4. PRACTICAL OPERATING APPLICATIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(i) Standby / Emergency Power Plants:
    Installed in hospitals, data centers, airports, and factories during main grid failure.
(ii) Peak Load Plants:
    Operated during brief daily peak demand intervals due to rapid start capability (within 2 to 5 minutes).
(iii) Base Load Plants for Remote / Isolated Grids:
    Ideal for isolated islands, hilly terrains, and mines where grid transmission line construction is uneconomical.
(iv) Nursery Stations:
    Installed in developing areas to build up electrical load until high-voltage grid lines arrive.
