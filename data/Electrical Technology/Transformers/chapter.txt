1. DEFINITION & GENERAL CHARACTERISTICS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Transformer is an AC machine that:
• Transfers electrical energy from one electric circuit to another.
• Does so without a change in frequency.
• Does so by the principle of electromagnetic induction.
• Has electric circuit that are linked by a magnetic circuit.

Transformers require no moving parts, hence called as static transformer:
• Due to which it has minimum maintenance.
• No losses due to moving parts (i.e. no friction and windage losses).
• Have the maximum efficiency:
  - Typical transformer efficiencies at full load lie between 96% to 97%.
  - Extremely large transformers have efficiencies up to 99%.


2. MAJOR COMPONENTS OF A TRANSFORMER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Transformer has three main components:
(i) Primary Winding:
    • Transformer’s primary windings when coupled to an electrical source, results in the production of magnetic flux.
(ii) Magnetic Core:
    • The magnetic flux that is produced by the main winding, will flow through this low-reluctance path linked to the secondary winding, which will result in the formation of a closed magnetic circuit.
(iii) Secondary Winding:
    • When the flux that is created by the primary winding passes through the core, it will eventually connect up with the secondary winding.
    • This winding generally winds on the same core as the other windings, and it provides the output that is initiated from the transformer.


3. WORKING PRINCIPLE & LAWS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Working Principle of a Transformer:
• The basic principle behind working of a transformer is the phenomenon of mutual induction between two windings linked by common magnetic flux.
• The transformer is based on two principles:
  1. An electric current can produce a magnetic field (electromagnetism).
  2. A changing magnetic field within a coil of wire induces a voltage across the ends of the coil (electromagnetic induction). Changing the current in the primary coil changes the magnetic flux that is developed. The changing magnetic flux induces a voltage in the secondary coil.

Working Flow:
• An alternating voltage source is connected with the primary winding which will produce a varying magnetic field when a current i flows through it.
• Now when we bring the secondary winding near to the primary, the changing magnetic field will link to it and according to Faraday's Laws of electromagnetic induction an EMF E will be induced in the secondary side.
• The flux produced is alternating EMF (the direction of it is continuously changing), gets induced in the secondary winding. This EMF is called 'mutually induced EMF', and the frequency of mutually induced EMF is same as that of supplied EMF.
• If the secondary winding is closed circuit, then mutually induced current flows through it, and hence electrical energy is transferred from one circuit (primary) to another circuit (secondary).
• The flux of the iron core links with the primary coil also, hence producing a self induced EMF in the primary winding. This self induced primary EMF opposes the applied voltage (sometimes called Back EMF of primary).


4. USEFUL FLUX VS. LEAKAGE FLUX
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
• Useful Flux:
  The core provides magnetic path for the flux, to get linked with the secondary winding. Most of the flux gets linked with the secondary winding which is called as 'useful flux' or main 'flux'.
• Leakage Flux:
  It is seen that the magnetic field lines generated due to the primary coil are not able to link completely with the secondary coil, so the losses are more. Thus a common magnetic medium is provided to link both coils in an effective way. The flux which does not get linked with the secondary winding is called as 'leakage flux'.


5. CORE MATERIAL AND CONSTRUCTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
• Material Requirements:
  The material used for core building must be magnetic material with high relative permeability to give low reluctance path for magnetic flux, so it can collect most of the flux produced by the primary winding.
• Silicon Steel:
  The magnetic core is made up of high grade silicon steel. Silicon steel reduces hysteresis loss.
• Laminated Construction:
  The core is made up of thin sheets called laminations (0.35 mm to 0.5 mm thick) coated with insulating varnish. The laminations reduce eddy current losses.


6. CLASSIFICATION OF TRANSFORMER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Transformers are classified into the following categories:

(i) Types of transformer based on the transformer's core:
    • Core Type Transformer
    • Shell Type Transformer
(ii) Types of transformer based on voltage conversion:
    • Step Up Transformer (V₂ > V₁, N₂ > N₁)
    • Step Down Transformer (V₂ < V₁, N₂ < N₁)
(iii) Types of transformer based on its purpose:
    • Power Transformer (> 200 kVA, maximum efficiency at full load)
    • Distribution Transformer (up to 200 kVA, high all-day efficiency)
    • Isolation Transformer (1:1 ratio for safety)
    • Current Transformer (CT, steps down heavy current for ammeters)
    • Potential Transformer (PT, steps down high voltage for voltmeters)
(iv) Transformer types based on windings:
    • Two Winding Transformer
    • Autotransformer (single continuous winding)
(v) Transformer types based on the insulation used:
    • Dry Type Transformer
    • Oil Immersed Transformer
(vi) Transformer types based on the number of phases:
    • Single Phase Transformer
    • Three Phase Transformer

Core Type vs. Shell Type Transformer:
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Feature                 Core Type Transformer           Shell Type Transformer
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Core Structure          Two limbs and two yokes         Three limbs (central + two outer limbs)
Windings                Windings surround core limbs    Core surrounds/encloses windings
Flux Path               Single closed magnetic loop     Flux divides into two paths (Φ/2 each)
Central Limb Area       Uniform across all limbs        Central limb area is twice outer limbs
Winding Placement       LV inside, HV outside on limbs  LV and HV sandwiched on central limb
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------


7. IDEAL TRANSFORMER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
An Ideal Transformer is a theoretical machine with 100% efficiency.
Characteristics of Ideal Transformer:
• No winding resistance (R₁ = 0, R₂ = 0 ⇒ Copper loss I²R = 0)
• No magnetic leakage (100% flux links both windings)
• No iron loss (Zero hysteresis and zero eddy current loss)
• Zero magnetising current (Infinite permeability)

Governing Equations:
• Induced EMFs:
  E₁ = 4.44 f Φ_m N₁  volts
  E₂ = 4.44 f Φ_m N₂  volts
• EMF per turn:
  E₁ / N₁ = E₂ / N₂ = 4.44 f Φ_m
• Transformation Ratio (K):
  K = V₂ / V₁ = E₂ / E₁ = N₂ / N₁ = I₁ / I₂
• Power Conservation:
  V₁ I₁ = V₂ I₂  (Apparent power input = Apparent power output)
