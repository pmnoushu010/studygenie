# Physics Chapter 6: Forces and Laws of Motion (Part 2)

## Conceptual Questions
1. State Newton's Second Law of Motion in terms of mass and acceleration.
2. Define one newton (1 N) of force.
3. Why does a cricket fielder gradually pull his hands backward while catching a fast-moving ball? Explain this phenomenon using Newton's second law.
4. How do airbags in vehicles help minimize passenger injuries during a sudden collision?
5. Define momentum. How is the more complete form of Newton's second law expressed in terms of momentum?
6. State Newton's Third Law of Motion.
7. Explain how a person is able to walk forward on the ground. Identify the action and reaction forces involved.
8. When rowing a canoe, how does pushing the water backward with a paddle cause the boat to move forward? Explain using Newton's third law.
9. Why are grooves provided on the soles of footwear and treads on vehicle tyres? (Referencing Bridging Science and Society)
10. If two identical spring balances are connected hook-to-hook and pulled in opposite directions (Activity 6.6), what will their readings show in relation to one another? What does this demonstrate?
11. Why are fragile glass items packed for transportation using bubble wrap or hay?

## Physics Problem-Type Questions
1. **Force Calculation ($F=ma$):** A car of mass 1200 kg accelerates uniformly at $3\text{ m/s}^2$. Calculate the net force acting on the car.
2. **Acceleration Due to Gravity:** What is the gravitational force (weight) acting on an object of mass $5\text{ kg}$ near the surface of the Earth? (Take $g = 9.8\text{ m/s}^2$).
3. **Holding a Barbell (Example 6.4 Variation):** A weightlifter is holding a barbell steady. The bar itself has a mass of 15 kg, and there is a 20 kg weight plate fixed on each side. How much upward force must the weightlifter apply to keep the barbell steady?
4. **Friction, Force, and Acceleration (Example 6.5 Variation):** A student is pushing a stationary block of $40\text{ kg}$ on a horizontal floor. The maximum force of friction opposing the motion is $60\text{ N}$. 
   - (i) If the student pushes with a force of $60\text{ N}$, what is the acceleration of the block?
   - (ii) If the student pushes with a constant forward force of $100\text{ N}$, what will be the acceleration of the block?
5. **Constant Velocity (Pause and Ponder 6):** A toy car of mass $200\text{ g}$ is moving in a straight line with a constant velocity of $1.5\text{ m/s}$. What is the net force acting on the toy car?
6. **Mass and Acceleration (Pause and Ponder 7):** Two children of masses $30\text{ kg}$ and $45\text{ kg}$ are sitting on identical swings. To impart identical initial acceleration, to which child would you need to apply a larger force? Explain your reasoning mathematically.
7. **Velocity-Time Graph Application (Example 6.6 Variation):** A $2000\text{ kg}$ truck's velocity-time graph shows a straight line inclined to the time axis. Its velocity increases from $0\text{ m/s}$ to $20\text{ m/s}$ in $10\text{ s}$. 
   - (i) Calculate the acceleration of the truck.
   - (ii) Calculate the force acting on the truck during this period.
8. **Action and Reaction:** An ice skater of mass $50\text{ kg}$ pushes off a wall with a force of $150\text{ N}$ to the East. What is the magnitude and direction of the force exerted by the wall on the ice skater?
