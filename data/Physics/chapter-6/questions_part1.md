# Physics Chapter 6: Forces and Laws of Motion (Part 1)

## Conceptual Questions
1. Define balanced and unbalanced forces. What is the effect of an unbalanced force on an object's motion?
2. A stalled car is being pushed by two people in the same direction. How do you calculate the magnitude of the net force acting on the car?
3. Describe the four main forces acting on a heavy box that is being pushed horizontally across a floor. 
4. Why does a bicycle gradually slow down and stop if you stop pedaling?
5. Explain the force of friction. In which direction does it act relative to the motion of an object?
6. How does the nature of the surfaces in contact affect the force of friction? (Referencing Activity 6.1 and 6.2)
7. According to Galileo's thought experiment, what would happen to a moving object on a perfectly smooth horizontal plane if all impediments to its motion were removed?
8. State Newton's First Law of Motion.
9. What is 'inertia'? Which scientist first used this word to describe the tendency of objects to resist change in their state of motion?
10. What does it mean for an object to have a constant velocity? 

## Physics Problem-Type Questions
1. **Net Force (Opposite Directions):** Two forces of 15 N and 8 N are acting on a block in opposite directions. Calculate the magnitude of the net force and state the direction in which the block will tend to move.
2. **Net Force (Same Direction):** Two people apply forces of 12 N and 14 N in the same direction to push a heavy cabinet. What is the total net force acting on the cabinet?
3. **Balanced Forces:** An object is being pushed with an applied force of 20 N to the right. The force of friction acting against it is exactly 20 N to the left. Calculate the net force. Will the object's state of motion change?
4. **Newton's First Law:** A net force of 0 N acts on an object that is currently moving in a straight line with a velocity of 5 m/s. What will be its velocity after 10 seconds? Explain your answer.
5. **Friction and Spring Balance:** A wooden block is pulled by a spring balance on a horizontal table. The reading on the spring balance is 7 N at the exact moment the block just starts moving. What is the magnitude of the force of friction acting between the wooden block and the table?
6. **Example 6.1 Variation:** Three identical blocks are placed on a table. 
   - Block A is pulled with 12 N to the left and 5 N to the left.
   - Block B is pulled with 10 N to the right and 10 N to the left.
   - Block C is pulled with 18 N to the right and 6 N to the left.
   Calculate the net force on each block and state whether the forces are balanced or unbalanced.
