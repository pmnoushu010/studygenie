# Physics Chapter 6: Forces and Laws of Motion (Part 3)

## Conceptual Questions
1. How can you simplify the calculation of acceleration for a system of two boxes connected by a string and pulled by a horizontal force? What are considered internal and external forces in this system?
2. If a table is moved across a floor at a constant velocity using a horizontal applied force $F$, how much is the frictional force exerted by the floor on the table? Explain your reasoning.
3. For a ball moving on a perfectly smooth, frictionless surface:
   - What happens to its velocity if no net force is applied?
   - What happens to its velocity if a net force is applied in the direction of its motion?
   - What happens if the net force is applied in the opposite direction?
4. A sailor jumps forward out of a small boat to the shore. In which direction will the boat move, and why?
5. During a high jump event, a soft landing mat or sand bed is placed for the athlete to fall upon. Explain the scientific reason behind this based on Newton's laws (specifically momentum and time of impact).
6. A fully loaded handcart collides with an identical but completely empty handcart. How do the magnitudes of the forces they exert on each other during the collision compare?
7. When the pole of a bar magnet is brought close to a magnetic compass, they exert equal and opposite forces on each other (Newton's third law). However, the compass needle visibly moves, whereas the heavy bar magnet does not. Explain why this happens.

## Physics Problem-Type Questions
1. **System of Objects:** Two boxes of masses $m_1 = 5\text{ kg}$ and $m_2 = 3\text{ kg}$ are placed on a frictionless horizontal surface and connected by a string. A horizontal force $F = 16\text{ N}$ pulls Box 1 to the right. By treating the two boxes as a single system, calculate the acceleration of the system.
2. **Net Force Analysis:** Block P has a $5\text{ N}$ force pushing it to the right and a $4\text{ N}$ force pushing it to the left. Block Q is moving with a constant velocity. Which of the two blocks experiences a non-zero net force? Justify your answer.
3. **Snake Boat Race:** While practicing for a snake boat race, 100 oarsmen are rowing a boat. 95 oarsmen row backwards to propel the boat forward, while 5 oarsmen row in the opposite direction by mistake. If each oarsman applies a horizontal force of $200\text{ N}$, what is the total net force on the snake boat? (Ignore drag forces).
4. **Graph Analysis:** An object of mass $10\text{ kg}$ is moving along a straight line. Its velocity-time graph shows velocity increasing uniformly from $10\text{ m/s}$ to $30\text{ m/s}$ over a time interval of $8\text{ seconds}$. Calculate the net force acting on the object.
5. **Stopping Force of a Bullet:** A bullet of mass $50\text{ g}$ ($0.05\text{ kg}$) moving with a speed of $100\text{ m/s}$ enters a heavy stationary wooden block and stops after penetrating a distance of $50\text{ cm}$ ($0.5\text{ m}$). Use kinematic equations to find its acceleration, and then estimate the stopping force acting on the bullet.
6. **Impulse and Contact Time:** An ace footballer converted a penalty shot by kicking a stationary football, giving it a speed of $108\text{ km/h}$ (Convert this to m/s first!). The estimated force imparted was $800\text{ N}$. The mass of the football is $0.4\text{ kg}$. Calculate the exact time of contact between their foot and the ball.
7. **Stopping Distance with Multiple Opposing Forces:** An object of mass $2\text{ kg}$ moving with a constant velocity of $10\text{ m/s}$ encounters a rough patch where the force of friction is $7\text{ N}$. At the exact same time, an additional constant force of $3\text{ N}$ opposing the motion is applied on the object. After entering the rough patch, what is the total distance the object travels before finally coming to rest?
